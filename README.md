# Brume

A browser prototype for laying out fog from utility passes.

Each shot's utility EXR (world position `P`, normals `N`, alpha, and a beauty or surface colour pass) is all Brume needs. It:

- raymarches fog straight against the plate, with per-pixel holdouts from `P`
- rebuilds the set from `P` as a rough mesh or point cloud you can orbit with Maya navigation (Alt + LMB / MMB / RMB); **Reset to shot cam** returns to the shot camera
- reads the ground, hollows, cold-air drainage lines, walls, tree canopy, slope direction and sun shade from the passes, so each fog type forms where and how it does in nature:
  - **Ground fog** (radiation fog): densest at the ground, collects in hollows and drainage lines, thin by walls and under trees; burn-off lifts it and leaves it lingering in shade
  - **Valley fog**: fills the connected basin under a soft top, with waves when wind runs over it; lifts off the ground as it burns off
  - **Low cloud** (stratus / hill fog): a broken deck with a ragged base that dips into gullies and drops on windward slopes, clears in the lee, with scud underneath
  - **Fog bank** (advection fog): streaked along the wind, rides over rising ground, thins pouring down lee slopes
  - **Rising mist**: wisps and puffs off wet forest canopy or ground
  - thin **mist**
- builds every element as a signed density field with detail octaves pushed in and out of its surface (Worley billows or fBm wisps, each octave warped by the last), faded by pixel footprint so distance never turns to speckle
- gives every element its own look: style presets per type, plus wisps ↔ billows, detail, breakup, windswept, turbulence, edge softness and structure size. Pick a tool and set the look before you place; select an element afterwards to change it
- **Settle** (S, or Settle all) lets elements come to rest the way real fog would: valley fog refills its basin from the ground up, ground fog and mist drain downhill into hollows, banks sit back on the ground, low cloud drops until the hills reach into it
- a Maya-style gizmo on the selected element: drag the fog to put it where you point, axis arrows, a height arrow, a rotate ring and resize handles on the outline. Resizing a terrain-derived element re-evaluates the ground instead of stretching it
- one **Fog brush**: pile up, thin out, add, erase or smooth fog in places (Ctrl does the opposite)
- **Draft / Final**: Draft is the fast interactive march; Final renders at full plate resolution with twice the steps, every detail octave and detailed self-shadowing, and keeps refining
- lights fog with a measured cloud-droplet (Mie) phase function, multiple scattering and soft sun shafts from the reconstructed set
- checks value structure (Values / Value bands) and copies a Nuke stub or Houdini VEX plus a fog-set JSON for hand-off

The shot menu is a stand-in for the sequence's shot list. It holds two generated test shots: `tst_0010`, a wide on a valley village, and `tst_0020`, a medium shot up a winding lane through a hillside village for smaller volumes. Dropping a utility EXR on the view still loads it (multipart scanline, NONE / RLE / ZIPS / ZIP); the camera comes from EXR metadata when present, or is solved from `P`. Files never leave your machine.

Live: https://x14halo.github.io/brume/

## Designer

`designer/` is the rebuild: a black void for designing one volume at a time, then exporting it to Houdini.

- **Library** of 32 volume types, each with what it is, when to use it and what it needs: fog (radiation, valley, advection, rolling fog, sea of clouds, upslope, freezing, ice, evaporation), clouds (cap, banner, deck, scud, rain curtain, cloud mass), atmosphere (depth haze, light-shaft haze, interior haze, inversion layer), lingering (residual smoke, settled dust, spray mist), emitters (sulfur smoke, steam vent, smoke source), stylized (glowing mist, magic swirl) and sculptable shapes (fog bank, hanging mist, ground fog patch, mist veil, rising wisps, tendrils, smoke puff).
- **Stand-in ground** to design against (flat, slope, peak, valley, bowl, street, pool, lake). Fog rests on it, drains down it, flows over and along it, and it casts shadows. Fog types that form by a rule (a layer that pools in hollows, fill to a level, upslope on the windward side, stacked sheets, a deck, a lid, even haze) form relative to it.
- **Emitters**: draw the source on the ground (disc, rectangle, line, ring) with the Source tool, then set soft edge, patchiness, pulse, rate, push, heat, spread, lifetime, tail fade and source fade. Plumes rise, level off as they cool and bend over in the wind.
- **Two modes.** **Shape**: the type forms once, then holds still for you to sculpt; nothing re-simulates while you work. **Animate**: your shape is frame 1 and the fog moves on from it with its own settings (wind in m/s, direction, gusts, churn, rise or sink, Hold). Play loops back to the shape instantly, and changing animation settings never changes the shape.
- **Animation box fitted to the shot**: on entering Animate, and whenever the timeline range or the motion changes, a quick low-resolution run from the shape over In–Out measures how far the fog actually goes, and the animation box is cut to that. Use only the first part of a growing plume and the box stays small. Setting the box by hand keeps it until Room goes back to Auto.
- **Influencers** to direct the motion in Animate, drawn in the view: **Wind path** (P; press where the air starts, on the fog or the ground, and draw: the path lies on a sheet through that point facing you, so a loop drawn from the side curls up and over; Shift draws level; Width is how far around the path the fog is carried; only a few well-placed points are kept; each path can also Twist the fog around itself), **Lift** (U; Ctrl sinks), **Calm** (C), **Thin** (T; Ctrl thickens), **Vortex** (S; Ctrl clockwise; drawn as a 3D spiral with dots to make it taller or wider). Every influencer drops a dashed line to the ground so its height reads at a glance. **Edit** (E): click to select and tune it in the panel, drag its dots to move them (Shift up and down, Ctrl the whole thing), Delete removes it.
- **Sculpt guidance**: the way you pulled, combed and blew the shape carries on gently as it plays (Follow my sculpt), so the motion continues from your sculpt rather than fighting it. Rise and sink are in metres per second.
- **Any value**: click the number on any slider to type a value, including past the slider's range.
- **Start from this frame** / **Sculpt this frame**: take any animation frame (pre-roll included) as the new shape, sculpt it, and the animation plays on from there. It is kept as a step in the shape's history, so it rebuilds exactly and undoes.
- **Physical daylight**, defaulting to a 10:30 am sun and sky. A low sun shines through more air, so it is dimmer, and the sky darkens with it. Manual keeps sun and sky as set.
- **Looks** in one click (soft mist, wispy curling smoke, puffy dense smoke, billowy, torn, streaky), plus Curl for how curly the detail is and **Fill gaps** to close holes so the fog reads as one volume.
- **Sculpt the fog you see**: Add (takes on the density and breakup of the fog it is painted onto), erase, smooth, Pull (grabs the fog under the brush, detail and all, and moves it with the cursor; Strength sets how closely), Comb (drags uneven strands through it), tear, Fill (fills holes and patches) and **Blow** (B: a gust along your stroke that carries the fog and curls it, using the real flow). Strokes are kept in order and replayed if the shape forms again. Undo and Redo sit on the viewport.
- **Painted looks**: up to three entries, each a full look (soft mist, torn fog, puffy smoke…). Paint an entry onto parts of the fog with the Paint look brush (L); change the entry's look later and everything painted with it follows. The painted looks travel with the fog as it moves, and show in their colours while the brush is active (Show colours turns that off).
- **Hide handles** (eye button, or H): only the volume, nothing drawn over it.
- **Shading in pipeline terms**: extinction distance, extinction colour, albedo and albedo colour, eccentricity (+0.85 for mist, negative for thick black smoke), scatter bounces (Auto = -1) and emission (off by default). The values go out with the export.
- **Box**: drag the dot on any side of the box (Box tool, X, or Look around) to move that side in or out; the fog is resampled, and a tighter box gives finer voxels. **Edge falloff** (Shading) fades the fog out towards the box walls so it never ends abruptly. The measured box ignores stray traces that hold a tiny share of the fog at an edge.
- **Real sizes** in three scale classes. On Auto the simulation box is measured, not guessed: a quick low-resolution run of the whole timeline (to Out) finds where the fog actually goes and the box is cut to that, plus a few voxels. A fixed voxel budget is then spent on that box, so a tighter box means finer voxels. Locked keeps the box as it is.
- **Detail level** per asset (Soft, Medium, Detailed, Hero, Auto, or **Custom**: type a voxel count for one axis and the others follow so voxels stay cubic, or set the voxel size) sits at the top of the panel, because voxels are what set a volume's detail: it sets the sim grid, detail octaves and export resolution, and shows the live sim voxel count, metres per voxel, export size and render weight.
- **Viewport resolution** 1K, 2K or 4K (long edge, top right of the view; G cycles). At rest the view renders every detail octave with detailed self-shadowing, refined over many light frames so the UI stays responsive; 2K and 4K are supersampled down to the screen.
- **One simulation per change**: the box is measured first with a quick low-resolution run, then the fog simulates once in it. Tightening or growing the box afterwards resamples the fog instead of simulating it again.
- **Timeline**: scrub the simulation, set In and Out on the stretch you like, set frames and fps, retime (slow motion re-simulates in finer steps rather than blending frames), extend a range for a longer shot, or Hold so the fog churns in place.
- **How it moves** (Animate): **Still** (only the detail moves), **Simulate** (the default: wind, gusts, churn, rise, **Drain downhill**, influencers, blockers, colliders and movers), **Simple** (Breathe, Roll or Drift the whole shape with an Amount and a Cycle, no simulation: it scrubs instantly and can loop) or **Still**. Drawing a force or picking a mover switches to Simulate. Every element starts out simulating the way it really moves (a fog bank rolls and drifts, a banner cloud streams, wisps rise and swirl, valley fog churns slowly and drains), a little livelier than life so it reads in a short shot; dial it back from there.
- **How the detail moves**, separately from the shape: **Pinned** (rides with the fog), **Boil** (each size of detail drifts past the others, so wisps slowly change shape), **Swirl** (small eddies turn it over in place), **Roll** (turns over along a direction) or **Stream** (combed along a direction). **Speed** in m/s, **Small detail** (how much livelier the small wisps are than the big ones; in real air they turn over faster), Strength and Direction.
- **Pre-roll** on the timeline (a second by default): the shaded part before frame 1, set in frames, and **Play from the start** plays it too, so the fog is already moving when the shot starts. **Every frame is kept as it plays** (the blue line under the bar), within a memory budget, so playing or scrubbing back is a copy, not a re-simulation.
- **Snappy view**: while you orbit or it plays, the volume draws at a resolution that keeps it smooth (fewer octaves, no fine self-shadowing) and sharpens when you stop; at rest it refines a band of the frame at a time, so the panels never stall.
- **Sim resolution** (Animation panel): Full (the default), Medium (about a quarter of the work) or Light (about a sixteenth): the motion simulates on a coarser grid while the fine wisps are still drawn on top, riding on it. Export simulates again at Full unless you choose *As previewed*. **Play from the start** sits next to Play (Shift+Space). The wind carries its eddies along with it, as real air does, so drifting fog keeps churning instead of sliding through still swirls.
- **Loop**: with Still or Simple, every motion is rounded to whole cycles over In–Out, so the last frame flows into the first.
- **No smearing**: the detail rides on two rest fields (where each bit of fog started) that start over in turn and cross-fade, so on a long shot the wisps never stretch into streaks. They are kept as offsets from the fog's own position, so half floats stay exact far from the origin and fine detail never jitters.
- **Detail**: **Billows** and **Wisps** as two amounts, **Billows toward** the top or the base (wispy underneath, billowy on top), **Core ↔ edge** (a thin soft rim round a dense core, or dense right to a defined edge), and **Heights from the ground** so fades and the billow blend follow the terrain.
- **Brushes land on what you see**: a stroke starts on the fog's visible surface, wispy edges included; just off the silhouette it finds the nearest fog around the cursor. The brush ring sits on the fog under the cursor. A stroke that needs room grows the box only as far as it must, and the box is trimmed back to the fog when you let go. **Blow** acts like a puff of air: the jet carries on past your stroke, widening and slowing, so the fog ahead is pushed on instead of piling up.
- **Shapes** (J): spheres, capsules, boxes and curves that block out the form; the fog forms from them with its noise and your strokes stay on top. Ctrl or Carve takes fog away. **Blockers** (O): boxes, spheres and walls the fog forms around and flows around, never into.
- **Fade near camera** (Shading), **Dark edges** and **Inner glow** (cloud lighting closer to a path tracer), and an **HDRI** (.hdr) behind the fog that lights it with its sky; *Sun from HDRI* puts the key light where its sun is.
- **Close-up** (view toolbar): drag a box over part of the frame; while it plays, that part renders at full quality and the rest holds still.
- **Scrubbing a simulation back** starts from the nearest kept frame instead of frame 1.
- **Motion looks** (Animate, Simulate): **Natural** (how that element really moves), **Pouring sea of clouds** (the smooth, layered marine layer of a mountain timelapse, pouring through gaps and down valleys in long rolling waves), **Rolling timelapse** (a dense, heavy river of fog rolling across the land and pouring down the slopes, its thin edges lifting off, breaking up and fading), **Dry-ice spill** (keeps welling up from the shape itself, like a fog machine, and runs like a liquid down every slope and into the hollows), **Slow drift** and **Lifting & burning off**. Every type in the library starts with its own motion and time speed, so fog that spills as it forms keeps spilling when it plays. The **Rolling fog** type is set up for the timelapse look (a bank on the brow of a hill with the wind taking it downhill), and **Sea of clouds** fills a deep valley under the peaks and pours along it.
- **Heavy** (on ground): dense fog runs down every slope like a liquid, funnels into gaps and pools in low ground, and never climbs; its thin edges can still lift. **Smooth flow** turns the churning down, lays the motion out in layers and draws the detail out into long streaks along the flow. A **Flowing sea** detail look goes with it.
- **Time**: Real time, 4×, 12×, 30× or 90× timelapse (the timeline's Speed still overrides it).
- **Heavy core, light edges**: **Wind pushes** and **Rise lifts / Sink pulls** go from *everything evenly* to *mostly the thin parts*, so wisps race ahead and rise while the dense core lags, low and heavy. **Edges & supply**: **Wisps lift off** (thin edges rise into the air by themselves and break up), **Thin edges fade** (they evaporate as they peel away; the core holds), **Keep arriving** (new fog keeps rolling in from the upwind side of the shape, in uneven pulses that ride the wind, so a bank streams past instead of thinning out; with no wind the shape keeps welling up), **Keep spilling** (the heavy fog keeps slumping out along the ground the way it did while forming).
- **Tabs, not a wizard**: Form · Look & light · Motion · Save (in the Shot: Set · Place & form · Look & light · Motion), every panel one click away. **Guide** shows the numbered steps with what to do in each.
- **Quick pill** under the selected volume: Density, Height, Breakup and Softness, right in the view.
- **Sun from the fog**: Shift+drag on the fog swings the sun behind that spot for a backlit rim; keep dragging to swing and raise it.
- **Weather pad** (Motion): drag one dot from calm and smooth to turbulent and gusty, and from heavy fog that pours and pools to buoyant fog that lifts and burns off. Every control is under **Advanced**.
- **Air brush** (R): drag a stream of air where the wind should carry the fog (a quick flick is strong, a slow drag gentle); Shift+drag lifts, Ctrl+drag makes still, dead air. It replaces separate Path, Lift and Calm tools (their shortcuts still work).
- **One wind for the shot**: a compass at the bottom left of the view (drag it) and Shot wind in Motion set one wind for every volume, slower near the ground and faster up high. Each volume follows it at its own strength (sheltered or exposed), or has its own.
- **No cookie-cutter edges**: types that form by a rule (radiation and ground fog, valley fill, layers, decks, haze) now form as a rounded, irregular patch with a wide soft falloff, so they blend into other volumes instead of ending in a square.
- **Place, size and turn in one go** (Shot): after picking a type, click to drop it as it is, or press where it goes and drag out: the drag sets its size, and its length lies along the drag. It then switches to **Move** (drag the ring to move, its edge to turn, the dot to scale), and the pill gains **Size** and **Turn**.
- **Time scale** per volume (Motion): every type plays at real speed by default. Slow one down so it barely moves, or speed another up so the wind pushes it more. It only changes how much time passes per frame: the simulation runs in the same small steps either way, so faster is never looser.
- **Reset all** (top bar): starts the whole tool over as on first launch, clearing every volume, shot, the Shelf and settings from this browser (it asks first; exported files are untouched).
- **Solo** (Shot, view toolbar): only the picked volume on the dark surround, with the same camera and sun. Click again for the whole set.
- **Shot camera inset**: orbit away from the shot camera and its frustum is drawn in the view, with a small picture of what it last saw in the corner; click it to go back.
- **Flood** (Y, Shot): press on the ground in a hollow, a valley or a street and drag up; a water line on the ground shows where the fog meets it and what pokes out. Let go and it fills to there, as a heavy valley fog.
- **Depth slab** (N, Shot): scroll to move a slab of depth nearer or further (the set inside it is tinted, its edges drawn), Shift+scroll to thicken it, then paint over the picture: mist fills that slab under your strokes and rests on the ground. [ ] sizes the brush.
- **Show the air** (Motion): while paused, faint streamlines show where the air carries the fog, through the same flow the simulation uses: wind, Air strokes, vortices, sources, ground and colliders.
- **Where it settles** (Forming, on ground): **Clings to shade** (it lingers out of the sun, behind hills and buildings and on shaded slopes, and burns off where the sun reaches the ground) and **Pools in hollows** (thicker in dips, thinner on ridges and steep ground), with no simulation.
- **Automatic collisions**: in a new shot the buildings and props in the scan collide by default (trees, water and the ground excepted); Pick (K) changes what the fog meets.
- **A/B compare** (view toolbar): hold what you see, change anything, then drag the line across the view to compare. **Playblast** (the record button by Play): every frame from In to Out at full quality, saved as a movie (MP4 or WebM) for review; Shift+click saves a PNG image sequence instead.
- **Brushes stay on the fog**: a painting stroke dragged into the distance follows the fog (or the ground) instead of floating off in front of it. Alt holds the stroke's depth.
- **Thin layers seen edge-on**: the renderer first finds where along each ray there is fog and spends its steps only there, so low ground fog from a low camera and sparse clouds keep their detail while you move.
- **Renderer-neutral shading in the USD**: every USD layer carries a material (/brume/Materials/<name>_fog) bound to the volume, a shader with info:id "BrumeVolume" holding the shading in physical terms (extinction, scattering and absorption per metre at density 1, albedo, anisotropy, emission), ready to map to your own renderer. The Houdini script only sublayers it; a Karma preview material is optional (KARMA_PREVIEW at the top of the script).
- **Fog source** (I, Animate): a box on the ground that keeps making fog and pours it out of one side, like a mass of fog rolling in. Press where it sits and drag out its width; set its height, depth, how fast it fills and how hard it pushes. It faces with the wind, or its own way. **+ Fog source on the upwind side** places one as wide and tall as your fog. With **Heavy** on, the fog it makes runs into the low ground and fills it: down a street, into a valley, through a gap.
- **Fill, then keep it**: run the motion (fast, as a timelapse if you like) to see where the fog naturally goes, stop on the frame you like and press **Keep this frame, barely moving**. That frame becomes the start, the sources go, and from there it only churns slowly at real speed, for short shots. **Start from this frame** and **Sculpt this frame** keep everything else as it was.
- **Scale & fine detail** (Detail): **Sense of scale** scales every detail size at once without touching the shape (finer detail reads as a much bigger thing), **Large ↔ small** sets which sizes of detail dominate, and **Add fine** lays an extra layer of small billows, wisps or grain on top at its own size and amount. The Houdini rebuild matches all three.
- **Scale guide** (view toolbar): faint ghost figures beside the fog, picked to suit its size from things everyone knows: a person, a car, a double-decker bus, a two-storey house, a big tree, a 10-storey building, the Statue of Liberty, the Eiffel Tower and the Empire State Building. Click to cycle through them, or turn it off.
- **Forming** develops properly: more Turbulence now churns the fog further rather than just finishing sooner, and forming always takes at least 16 steps.
- **Export for Houdini and Solaris**: OpenVDB files written directly (half-float density), a **USD layer** (a Volume prim with its density field per frame and Brume's shading as attributes), and `load_in_houdini.py`, which builds the SOPs and, in /stage, sublayers the USD with a Karma Pyro material carrying the shading. **Detail baked in** writes the fog exactly as you see it; **Rebuilt in Houdini** writes small files (the coarse fog, its two rest fields, the look per voxel and the noise tiles) plus a Volume Wrangle that rebuilds the same detail, moving the same way, at any resolution. In the Shot, a shot USD stacks every layer. Recipes save as JSON.

### Steps

Both workspaces run as a short series of steps across the top of the panel. Each step shows only the panels and tools it needs, says in a sentence what to do, and offers the next step. Tool shortcuts still work and move to their step. Every tool on the left rail has a label.

- **Shot:** 1 Scan (check the set) · 2 Ground (colliders) · 3 Place (add fog from the Library and click on the set) · 4 Look · 5 Sculpt · 6 Animate.
- **Designer:** 1 Start · 2 Look · 3 Sculpt · 4 Animate · 5 Save.

### Shot (test scene)

The **Shot** tab opens a made-up test shot (a village on a valley floor, rolling hills, a mountain range) through a shot camera. It works like the Designer, on the landscape:

- **+ Add fog** opens the same Library as the Designer. Pick a type and **click on the set**: it forms there against the real ground (ground fog drapes it, a cap settles on the peak you click, rolling fog and seas of clouds pour down the real slopes; things that float are lifted a little). Up to eight volumes, drawn together.
- **Every placed volume is a full Designer volume**: Start from (*Change type…* keeps it where it stands), Size (with **Above the ground**), Forming, Detail, Shading, painted looks, the sculpt brushes, and all of Animate (motion looks, Heavy, Fog sources, keep-frame). Pick the one to work on under **Fog in this shot**. **Move** (W) moves, turns and scales it; duplicates are instances.
- Volumes saved to the Designer's **Shelf** can be placed the same way.

Older shots with painted layers still load and draw:

- **Layers** (up to four), each a fog type that forms by its own rule relative to the ground: **Ground fog** (lies on the ground, set depth), **Valley fog** (fills the low ground to a level), **Hanging mist** (floats at a height above the ground), **Low cloud** (a layer at an altitude, wrapping the slopes it meets), **Mountain cap** (sits on the peaks you paint over), **Haze** (even, up to a height).
- **Place brush** (Q): pick a layer and paint on the ground where it goes; the ground is tinted with each layer's colour while you paint, and the fog forms there when you let go. Ctrl erases.
- **Each layer is its own volume**, with its own box, voxels and detail level, look and animation, so valley fog in the village keeps its detail next to cloud on the peaks. The layers are drawn together over the set, nearest last. The layer you pick is the one every Shape and Animate tool works on. **Shot camera** returns to the shot's framing.
- **Starts empty**: add a layer, then paint it in.
- **Shot layers start at Designer-level detail**: cubic voxels sized by type (about 1.2 m for ground fog, 1.6 m for valley fog, 2 m for hanging mist, 3.5 m for cloud and caps, 8 m for haze), held to roughly 7M voxels per layer. Change it per layer under Detail level.
- **The set is a scan**, as a Houdini bake would make it. Rays are cast from the shot camera, then from temporary cameras placed automatically around everything it sees (a ring at two heights, one far beyond looking back, one overhead). Every first hit is kept as a point with its position, normal, colour and object id, and overlaps are merged so extra cameras only add surface nobody else saw. Brume draws the points as small lit discs lying on their surfaces, with sun shadows. The fog layers read the scan's depth, and each object's path is stored once. In the browser the GPU rasteriser stands in for the ray casting.
- **Light by default:** the set is shown as a *Light* scan (about a tenth of the points, each one bigger), switchable to Full, Lightest or Hidden. While you move it is drawn at the fog's preview resolution. Fog layers use the Designer's own detail budget (Detailed; Medium for haze), so a layer has the same number of voxels across as a Designer volume.
- **What the fog sits on:** *Scanned ground* (plus anything you pick), *Only my picks*, or a *Flat floor* that ignores the set. Any of them is one small height image for the simulation.
- **Colliders: nothing collides unless you pick it.** Use the **Pick colliders** tool (K) in the viewport, or tick objects in the **scene tree** in the Set panel (or select rows and **Add selected**). Pick by **Element** (a whole house or tree) or **Leaf** (just a roof). Picked objects' proxy shapes become the fog's ground, highest surface wins. Where nothing is picked, the fog sits on the ground found in the scan: the lowest upward-facing points, with small things like houses and trees opened away.
- **Fill gaps** (Set panel): adds cameras only where they help. It finds the surfaces just behind what the shot camera sees (its rays' second hits), ignoring insides and buried parts. Then it aims cameras greedily at what is still unseen, checking each candidate by actually looking from it. It stops at 6 cameras, when what is left is small, or when two cameras in a row barely helped. *Fill gaps on every scan* turns it on for every rescan.
- **More cameras:** select a camera in the scene tree and choose *Scan from selected camera*, or choose *Scan from this view*. Only new surface is added, and the shot remembers those cameras for later scans.
- **Scenes:** *Village*, or *Motion test*: a meadow with a walker, a cantering horse, a car on the road, tumbling planks and a windmill, under a static camera.
- **Movers** (Animate): click something that moves, or tick it in the tree, and it pushes the fog as it moves. The same tool picks colliders and movers; Brume knows which objects are animated. Each mover becomes a small soft volume per frame recording how much of it is there and how fast it is moving. The fog is dragged along inside it, shoved aside at its edges and cleared from its core. A mover has two sliders: **Strength** (below zero it only clears a path) and **Simplify** (blurs it first, so thin parts like legs fade, then it becomes one soft blob).
- **Before frame 1**, each mover gets an automatic pre-roll: long enough to travel three or four of its own lengths. Its motion is guessed from its speed at the start (*Carry on*), or set to *Hold still* or *From the cache*. During the pre-roll the movers run through the sculpted fog, so frame 1 opens with their wake. In Animate, drag a mover's faint ghost to change where it came from.
- **Sphere movers** (M): click to place a sphere, then move the playhead and click or drag to key its path.
- **Move layer** (W): move, turn or scale a layer without repainting it (drag inside the ring, the ring, or the dot); it forms again where you put it. **Duplicate layer** copies it alongside. **Blockers** belong to the shot, so every layer flows around them.
- **Attract** (A, Animate): a black hole for fog. It has a **core** (the source, at full strength) and a **reach** (how far out it pulls), each with its own dot in the view. Fog is drawn in and can spin like a drain, with an optional **Consume** in the core. Ctrl, or a negative Pull, makes a blast that pushes fog outward from the core.
- **Scan file (.bset)**: the format a Houdini bake would write. It holds a JSON header (cameras, prim paths and kinds, proxy ranges) plus binary blocks: points (P f32×3, N i8×4, colour u8×4, id u32, radius f32) and proxy meshes. Save it from the Set panel and load it back.
